DEFINITION
----------
Polypharmacy is defined as the concurrent use of 5 or more medications. Hyperpolypharmacy refers to 10 or more concurrent medications. Both are associated with increased risk of adverse drug events (ADEs), drug-drug interactions, falls, cognitive impairment, hospitalizations, and mortality in older adults.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
------------
- Approximately 40% of adults aged 65+ take 5 or more medications.
- Patients with 5+ chronic conditions average 8–12 medications.
- Polypharmacy is independently associated with a 1.5-fold increased risk of falls and a 2-fold increased risk of adverse drug events.
- An estimated 30% of hospital admissions in elderly patients are medication-related.

DEPRESCRIBING FRAMEWORK
------------------------
Deprescribing is the planned, supervised process of dose reduction or discontinuation of medications that are no longer needed or may be causing harm.

Step 1: INVENTORY — List all medications including OTC, supplements, and PRN drugs.
Step 2: INDICATION — Verify each medication has a current, valid indication.
Step 3: RISK-BENEFIT — Assess whether the medication's risks outweigh benefits given the patient's current clinical status, life expectancy, and goals of care.
Step 4: PRIORITIZE — Rank medications for deprescribing based on:
   - Harm potential (Beers Criteria drugs first)
   - Drug-drug interaction burden
   - Pill burden and adherence difficulty
   - Therapeutic duplication
Step 5: PLAN — Taper gradually where appropriate (especially gabapentinoids, PPIs, beta-blockers). Monitor for withdrawal or rebound effects.
Step 6: MONITOR — Follow up within 2–4 weeks after each deprescribing action.

FALLS RISK AND MEDICATION REVIEW
---------------------------------
Patients presenting with falls should receive immediate medication review. High-risk combinations include:
- Gabapentin/pregabalin + beta-blocker (orthostatic hypotension + sedation)
- Loop diuretic + ACE inhibitor (volume depletion + hypotension)
- Anticoagulant + NSAID (bleeding risk amplification)
- Multiple CNS-active drugs (cumulative sedation)

For patients with ≥ 2 falls in 12 months OR 1 injurious fall, a comprehensive geriatric assessment including medication review is strongly recommended.

DIABETES IN THE ELDERLY — MEDICATION CONSIDERATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------
For older adults with type 2 diabetes:
- HbA1c target should be individualized: < 7.5% for healthy older adults, < 8.0–8.5% for those with multiple comorbidities, limited life expectancy, or high hypoglycemia risk.
- Avoid glyburide (long-acting sulfonylurea) — high hypoglycemia risk.
- Metformin remains first-line but requires renal monitoring (contraindicated if eGFR < 30).
- Insulin regimens should be simplified where possible (e.g., once-daily basal insulin rather than complex multi-dose regimens).
- GLP-1 receptor agonists offer benefits for weight and cardiovascular outcomes with low hypoglycemia risk.

HEART FAILURE AND POLYPHARMACY
-------------------------------
CHF patients are inherently polypharmacy-prone due to guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) requiring multiple agents (beta-blocker, ACEi/ARB/ARNI, diuretic, MRA). Additional medications for comorbidities compound the burden. Key considerations:
- NSAIDs are contraindicated in CHF (sodium and water retention, reduced renal perfusion).
- Gabapentin dose must be adjusted for renal function in CHF patients with CKD.
- Monitor for hypotension when combining multiple antihypertensives with diuretics.

QUALITY METRICS
---------------
Healthcare systems should track:
- Percentage of patients aged 65+ with ≥ 5 medications receiving annual medication review
- Percentage of patients on Beers Criteria drugs with documented risk-benefit assessment
- Fall rate in polypharmacy patients vs. non-polypharmacy patients
- Rate of medication-related hospital admissions
