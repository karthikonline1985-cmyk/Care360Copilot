SUMMARY
-------
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are widely recognized as carrying an increased risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) and can accelerate the progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD).

It is generally accepted in clinical practice that patients with pre-existing renal impairment, including CKD stages 2–5, are at substantially elevated risk of NSAID-induced nephrotoxicity.

AFFECTED MEDICATIONS
--------------------
All prescription and over-the-counter NSAIDs, including but not limited to:
- Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin)
- Naproxen (Aleve, Naprosyn)
- Diclofenac (Voltaren)
- Celecoxib (Celebrex)
- Meloxicam (Mobic)
- Indomethacin (Indocin)
- Ketorolac (Toradol)

Low-dose aspirin (81mg) for cardiovascular prophylaxis is not included in this warning.

MECHANISM OF RENAL INJURY
--------------------------
NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. Prostaglandins maintain afferent arteriolar vasodilation in the kidney, which is critical for maintaining glomerular filtration rate (GFR), particularly in patients with reduced renal perfusion. NSAID use causes afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, reducing renal blood flow and GFR.

In patients with normal renal function, this effect is usually clinically insignificant. However, in patients with CKD, heart failure, volume depletion, or concurrent use of ACE inhibitors/ARBs or diuretics, NSAID-induced vasoconstriction can precipitate acute kidney injury.

KEY FINDINGS (SYNTHETIC — ILLUSTRATIVE FIGURES)
-------------------------------------------------
1. Patients with CKD stage 3 or higher who used NSAIDs for more than 7 consecutive days had a 3.2-fold increased risk of AKI compared to non-users.
2. Concurrent use of NSAIDs with ACE inhibitors and diuretics (the "triple whammy" combination) increased AKI risk by 4.8-fold.
3. Even short-term NSAID use (3–7 days) in CKD patients was associated with a measurable decline in eGFR.
4. Over-the-counter NSAID use was identified as a contributing factor in 15% of preventable AKI hospitalizations in patients aged 65 and older.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR HEALTHCARE PROVIDERS
-----------------------------------------
1. AVOID prescribing NSAIDs to patients with CKD stage 3 or higher (eGFR < 60 mL/min).
2. If NSAID use is unavoidable, use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration.
3. Monitor serum creatinine and eGFR within 7 days of initiating NSAID therapy in at-risk patients.
4. Educate patients with CKD about the risks of over-the-counter NSAIDs.
5. Consider safer alternatives for pain management: acetaminophen, topical analgesics, physical therapy, or non-pharmacologic interventions.
6. Review the "triple whammy" risk: NSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic. Avoid this combination whenever possible.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PATIENTS
-----------------------------
- If you have kidney disease, do not take ibuprofen, naproxen, or other NSAIDs without consulting your healthcare provider.
- Check labels on over-the-counter pain relievers and cold medicines for NSAID ingredients.
- Acetaminophen (Tylenol) is generally considered safer for kidney patients when used at recommended doses.
- Report any decrease in urination, swelling, or unusual fatigue to your healthcare provider immediately.
